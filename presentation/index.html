We are solving for the weakest link in any technical system: the human being in it. Ninety seconds on why that is suddenly urgent, then I will show you the thing running.
The problem

Every layer of our stack got faster this year.

The human in it did not.

We have spent two years making the machine part of engineering faster. The part that understands the system is the same speed it always was — and it is now being asked to keep up with output it never wrote.
This is not a thesis we invented for a hackathon. Engineers have been saying it out loud, on Hacker News, all year.
Don't argue the point — hand it to the audience as something they already feel. Then move straight to the evidence wall; the headlines do the arguing.

It stopped being a feeling · it is measured

Two independent studies, two professions, the same result.

Endoscopists

The Lancet, 2025. After habituating to AI assistance, detection quality dropped when the assistance was taken away.

Programmers

Anthropic, 2026. The same shape: people who got used to the assistant performed worse on the same task without it.
Skill loss from AI assistance is no longer anecdote. It is the first measurable deskilling effect of this generation of tools.
Reported in Nature, 19 June 2026 — “Is AI ruining our skills? Early results are in — and they're not good”.
This is the slide that turns the room. Two professions you cannot dismiss — one of them is doctors. Same effect. Say the phrase "first measurable deskilling effect" slowly.
The name

Cognitive debt.

The gap between a system that keeps evolving and the understanding the people responsible for it actually hold.
Coined by Prof. Margaret-Anne Storey, and now in general circulation — it is the word engineers reach for when they describe what generative AI is doing to them. We named the project after it.
Debt is the right metaphor. It compounds silently, it is invisible until you need the money, and you can pay it down.
Important: we did not invent this term. A professor did, and the industry adopted it. That's why the product is called cogdebt — and the whole pitch is the last sentence: debt can be paid down. Nobody is doing that part.
What is on offer today

Every existing answer subtracts something.

Turn it off

“No AI Fridays” — one day a week with the assistants disabled.
Costs: a fifth of your velocity.

Be slower on purpose

Forbid the agent from touching files; retype every generated line by hand.
Costs: the reason you bought the tool.

Memorise

Spaced-repetition flashcards against “skill rot”.
Costs: recall is not understanding.
Three real products and practices, all from the same Hacker News cluster. All three treat the tool as the problem. Nobody is paying down the debt.
These are real: noaifridays.com, the retype-by-hand post, and fata.dev. Be generous about them — they're honest attempts. The point is the shape they share: subtraction. We add.
Our bet

Don't take the tool away.

Pay the debt with what the engineer already owns.

A senior engineer is not empty. They hold a deep, load-bearing model of some field. The fastest route into a new one is not a tutorial — it is a correspondence to the one they already have.

Douglas Hofstadter:
Analogy as the Core of Cognition

Analogy is not a garnish on thought — it is the interstate highway system of it. Categorisation is a consequence of analogy.
The method underneath is Gentner's structure mapping: correspondences are matched on the role a thing plays, never on what it is called.
The Hofstadter talk surfaced on HN two days ago — same archive as the problem slides. That's a nice line: the same community that named the disease also pointed at the cure.
Debt is a number, not a mood
debt(c) = frequency(c) × prereq_depth(c) × (1 − mastery(c))

frequency

How much you actually lean on it. Not self-report — we scan your public repositories and read it off your real work.

prereq_depth

How much else rests on it. A shaky foundation costs more than a shaky leaf.

1 − mastery

How little of it you hold — moved only by answers you actually gave, and damped so one lucky reply proves nothing.
Self-reported skills are flattering. A repository scan is not. That is what turns “I feel rusty” into a ranked list of what to fix first.
The GitHub scan is the bit that makes this honest. Everyone over-rates themselves; your commits do not. That's the input the formula was missing until we wired the scanner.
The core mechanic · match on role, not on name

Not “both have a controller”.

“Both are the single source of truth that everything else reconciles against.” That is a structural role — and it is what produces a mapping worth having.
etcd maps to feature store
shared role · source_of_truth
⚠ etcd is small and strongly consistent. A feature store is a materialized view over dirty events, split into an offline and an online path. Point-in-time correctness is an invariant etcd never had.
liveness probe maps to drift detection
shared role · degradation_signal
⚠ A probe is cheap, binary and contemporaneous. Labels arrive late, incomplete and biased — drift can look healthy while the business metric dies.
Real output. Point at the orange line, not the top line — the top line is what any chatbot gives you. The orange line is the product.
The one invariant

An analogy with no stated limit

does not pay down cognitive debt. It creates more.

Give someone a mapping and stop there, and they will carry the borrowed intuition straight past the point where it holds — and they will be confident while doing it. That is the failure mode of every “X is just Y” explanation ever given. 
// internal/domain — the pure core, enforced in code
func (m Mapping) Valid() error {
    if len(m.Breakdown) == 0 {
        return errors.New("a mapping with no breakdown creates debt instead of paying it")
    }

}
It is a required field on the domain type, a required field in the plugin schema, and a test asserts the agent's instruction still demands it. Three places, because this is the product.
This is the slide to slow down on. Everyone in the room has been given a bad analogy that they then over-applied. We made the limit mandatory at the type level.
Extraction, not explanation

Four rungs, chosen by code — never by the model.

Find the seam yourself. We do not name it for you — being told is not the same as noticing.
L3
On its own terms
Answer in the target field's vocabulary, with no analogy left to lean on.
L4
Put it together
A small real problem needing two or three target concepts at once — graded by running it.
Which concept, and which rung, is deterministic domain logic — driven by debt and measured mastery. Only the wording of the question is left to the model. A model asked to pick the difficulty and judge the answer drifts toward whatever it just explained.
This is the design decision I'm proudest of. The LLM writes sentences. It does not get to decide whether you passed, or what comes next. That's code, and it's testable.

The plugin ABI · the whole contract
type Extension interface {
    Manifest() Manifest
    Invoke(ctx, tool string,
           in json.RawMessage)
           (json.RawMessage, error)
    Close() error
}
Only bytes cross it. No pointers, no channels, no *sql.DB, no fyne.CanvasObject — which is exactly why the same plugin runs in-process or in its own OS process with no code change.
A plugin never calls another plugin. It declares what it Requires; the host resolves. The call graph stays flat.

If a judge is technical, this is the slide they'll ask about. Three methods, bytes only. And the analogy plugin is a JSON description of an agent — swap the instruction, swap the teaching strategy, no recompile.
Why a local desktop app

Because this is a proof of concept, and auth is not the point.

What we bought

No accounts, no sessions, no tenancy, no cloud bill. Your profile, your mastery and your analogies are one SQLite file on your own disk. Your unflattering skill assessment never leaves the laptop.

What we deferred

Sync, teams, a shared debt ledger for a whole org. All of it is a transport change behind the same ABI — the domain layer already knows nothing about where it runs.
The honest version: we spent the hackathon on the teaching loop, because that is the part nobody has solved. Login screens are solved.
Judges always ask "why not a web app". Answer it before they do, and say the real reason: we'd rather spend the time on the thing that's hard. And the privacy angle is genuine — a list of what you don't know is not something you want on someone else's server.

We only found it because every seam is traced with timings. The sub-agent was burning 4,069 tokens over 74 seconds on an instruction that asked for three short pairs — all of it reasoning, none of it visible, none of it useful.
An agent that follows a written procedure does not want a reasoning model. An agent that decides what to do next does. Ours are configured separately, per plugin, as one field in a manifest.
This is our "we actually engineered this" slide. 3.5x on the whole turn from one config field, found by tracing rather than guessing. If you have 20 seconds left, say the last sentence.
